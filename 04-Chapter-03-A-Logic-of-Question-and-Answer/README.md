## The big idea

"Logic" here doesn't mean formal symbolic logic (minding your *p*'s and *q*'s; that comes in Part II). It means **structure or order**: how questions and answers are arranged and how they move.

Chapter 3 gives the **positive account** of what we're conscious of when we question (its "sense"):

1. **Four orientations** for questioning a complete thought: predicate, subject, copula, and questioning itself.
2. The **content of questioning is *questionability*** (*Frageverhalt*), and questioning has **hermeneutic priority**: it is the *transcendental condition* for understanding anything.
3. **Prejudices** are historically transmitted presuppositions. They determine *which* questions can occur to us.
4. Without genuine questions, thought hardens into **dogma**, through the **hegemony of the sign**, **propositionalism**, and **substance metaphysics**.

## 1. How to question complete thoughts from at least four directions (pp. 45–49)

### First, what is the "sense" of a complete thought?

- **Sense** here isn't physical sensation. It is **meaning** in the broad sense: *a sense* of direction, of humor, of ritual, common sense.
- Our natural attitude pictures a proposition as **pointing** at a "this" and **ascribing a property** to it ("this is tall"). That reified, **representational** sense stacks "one picture on top of another," cut off from the "irrigation and fertilization" of genuine questioning (Gadamer).
- **Dickman's alternative:** the unity of meaning comes when **a predicate successfully *discloses* a subject's essence**. Subjects **"pass into"** their predicates.
  - **Gadamer:** "'God is one' does not mean that it is a property of God's to be one, but that it is God's nature to be [unified]."
  - "This tree is tall" doesn't attach tallness as a mere property. **We experience the tree *in its tallness*** ("That tree sure is tall!").
  - **Metaphor:** "Anderson is a lion." Taken literally it's nonsense. The literal unity **implodes** so a metaphorical unity can emerge, revealing aspects of Anderson's essence.
  - **"This year is 1442 AH"** reveals time *as what it truly is* for a community: coordinated with the founding of the *Ummah* in Medina.

### So what is the sense of a *question*?

It isn't simply the *separation* of subject and predicate (Schumann & Smith 1987).

> **Complete thoughts present the actual unity (or separation) of subject and predicate. Questions present the *possibilities* for unity (or separation).**

**Husserl:** every possible content of judgment can be thought as the content of a question. In the question it is only *contemplated*, a **neutralized judgment**, "oriented equally toward the yes and the no."

- **Genuine questioning neutralizes determined thoughts.**
- **Neutrality = suspension of judgment = suspension of *pre*-judice** (pre-judgment).
- **Suspension is neither assertion nor negation.** It comes before both, in a higher order.

### The four orientations

```
                         COMPLETE THOUGHT
            [ SUBJECT ] ──── [ COPULA ] ──── [ PREDICATE ]
                 ▲                ▲                ▲
                 │                │                │
     (2) Subject-centered  (3) Copula-centered  (1) Predicament-centered
       "What is for         "Should one really     "Where are my keys?"
        breakfast?"          treat others as…?"

                (4) Questioning-centered: the questioner's own abeyance
                    "What sort of person am I in my questioning?"
```

| # | Orientation | What's settled | What's in *abeyance* | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Predicament-centered** (predicate-centered) | The **subject** stands in relief like a teeter-totter's fulcrum | **Which predicate** fittingly discloses it | "Where are my keys?"; "What is the distance to New York City?" (precise answers exist; the question is *which* fits) |
| 2 | **Subject-centered** | The **predicate** stands out from a field of intelligibility | **Which subject** the predicate fittingly discloses. The interrogative works like an algebraic variable | "What is for breakfast?" (samosas? nothing yet?); "What gets wetter as it dries?" (umbrella, dryer, towel…) |
| 3 | **Copula-centered** | A **prior complete thought** is presupposed | The **being of the relation**: its *justification or grounds*. Is the predicate actually disclosive of the subject? | "Donald J. Trump is the president. But is he really?"; "Should one really 'treat others as you want to be treated'?" (convention? revelation? rational principle?) |
| 4 | **Questioning-centered** | — | **We ourselves**, as participants in questioning | "What sort of person am I in my questioning: open to others, or someone who already knows and needn't listen?" |

- Name source: Kant calls concepts "**predicaments**" and the basic categories "**predicables**."
- Each orientation has a **to-and-fro "teeter-totter"** play of indeterminacy.
- Copula-centered questions (Husserl) concern **preserving the reliability of our cognitive capacities**.

### Questioning-centered questioning, in depth

- Just as complete thoughts need a thinker, **questioning involves a questioner**. We can attend to ourselves *as participants* in questioning, not as its topic.
- **Merleau-Ponty:** "**questioning to the second power**." We disclose ourselves as a **negativity "borne by an infrastructure of being."**
- We aren't a mere "lack" or nothing. We're **suspended in productive abeyance** between negativity and positivity, multiplicity and unity.
- **Ricoeur:** this ambivalence is "**the ruin of the pretension of the ego to be established as an ultimate origin.**"
- Being in question **negates our need for dominance**, because we're opened to what others say (developed in Part III).
- **Gadamer:** questioning means "being transformed into a communion in which we do not remain what we were."
- **Irigaray:** questioning is an **opening in all of us**, from which we can **listen** to others.
- **Caveat:** asking genuine questions *in order to* transform yourself misses the point. That **instrumentalizes** them. **Transformation is a by-product.**
- Openness reworks not only experience and our interpretations of it, but **ourselves** (Beatty 1999).

### What about "sign-centered" questions?

- Could we question the **signs** that make up complete thoughts? Perhaps, but only as a **derivative** of the four.
- The four orientations assume questioner and answerer **share a form of life and a language**.
- Sign-centered questions arise when **that sharing breaks down**. Someone says "**Thank.**" — "'Thank' what? 'Thank you'? 'Thankful'?" These questions try to place a decontextualized sign in context (as a predicate with an implied subject, or vice versa).
- They're **sincere, not genuine**. **Genuine questioning gets off the ground once a shared language is established.**

## 2. Presuppositions provide constraints in questions (pp. 49–54)

### Intentionality

- **Intentionality** is the built-in structure of consciousness: a **noesis** (the subjective origin) and a **noema** (the meant target). Consciousness is always conscious *of* some meaning.
- **De Beauvoir:** intentionality is our original attachment to being. It is not a "wanting to *be*" but a "**wanting to *disclose***." Her landscape passage: she'd like to *be* the sky and water, yet it's the *distance* that lets them exist before her. Contemplation is excruciating only because it is also a joy.
- **Questioning makes present the fluidity of subject matters radiating predicative possibilities, and of predicates radiating possible subjects.**
- Our **structural negativity** (subjectivity isn't an object) **clears space** for things to appear *in their questionability*.
- **Gadamer (tr. Coltman):** "Such bringing-into-suspension … is the proper and original essence of questioning. Questioning always allows the possibilities of a situation to be seen in suspension."

### Suspension, not closure

- The natural attitude assumes questioning aims at **cognitive closure**.
- **Suspension** lets us **dwell** with varying possibilities, exploring them over time without having to assume any one.
- Subjects and predicates may each be determinate while their **possible relations** stay open. **Image:** the number continuum. Each number can be brought into relief and then recedes into the "ocean of infinity."

### The objective correlate: *Frageverhalt*

- A question isn't just an introspective report of my confusion. If it were, **we could just frown** instead of asking.
- **Johannes Daubert's** distinctions within ***Sachverhalt*** ("subject matter" or state of affairs):

| German | Literal meaning | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| ***Erkenntnisverhalt*** | known subject matter | the state of affairs **as cognized** |
| ***Frageverhalt*** | questioned subject matter | the state of affairs **as in question** |
| ***Wunschverhalt*** | wished-for subject matter | the state of affairs **as wished** |

- **The objective correlate of questioning is an "opening up" of a structure in subject matters, like a pop-up book**, unfolded by separating yet suspending subjects and predicates.
- **The intentional object of questioning is the *questionability* of the object.** Asking a question expresses something's *questionability*, just as declaring a judgment expresses its *knowability*.
- Questioning *may* (but **need not**) try to move from an open *Frageverhalt* to a closed *Erkenntnisverhalt*.

### Hermeneutic priority

> **Gadamer:** "To understand a question is to ask it, but to understand a meaning is to understand it as an answer to a question."

- Only by asking can a **perceived sentence** become an **understood complete thought**.
- **Only statements can mean something, and only if they answer questions actually asked.**
- **Understanding a meaning ≠ meaning it.** We can consider a complete thought as one possible answer among many. To *mean* it takes **appropriation**.
- With questions it's different. **The only way to "understand" or "mean" a question is to ask it.**
  - **Gadamer:** there's no "tentative or potential attitude of questioning." Saying "such and such question might arise" is already real questioning, **masked by caution or politeness**.
- **We can decide whether to voice a question, but not whether it occurs to us.** Getting caught up in questioning is closer to a **passivity**, Levinas's "**nonintentional consciousness**." Questions *occur* to us or *strike* us. **This *occurrent* (not ego-driven) character marks genuine questioning.**

**The parallel:**

```
sentences            = complete thoughts perceived
complete thoughts    = sentences understood
interrogative sents. = questions perceived
asked questions      = interrogative sentences understood
```

### "What did I just read?!"

You finish a page of a difficult book and realize you understood nothing. Set aside boredom, distraction, and jargon. In the cases where you were attentive and fluent and *still* lost, the fundamental cause is:

> **We weren't actively asking the questions the text answers.** Sentences don't float in "ontological outer space." They answer specific questions.

### Three kinds of priority

| Kind of priority | Example | Can it be undermined? |
|---|---|---|
| **Logical presuppositions** (erotetic logic): implicit lists of options | "Vanilla or chocolate?" → "Select one: I want vanilla. I want chocolate." "What's your name?" assumes you *have* a name | **Yes.** Loaded assumptions ("Have you stopped beating your dog yet?" assumes a dog, beating, and an obligation to stop) can be **rejected** |
| **Chronological / content constraints**: in *genuine* questioning, presuppositions give **parameters pointing toward sources** of answers | — | **Yes.** They "flutter." Readers often reach the question *after* the sentences |
| **Hermeneutic priority**: **questioning is a condition of the possibility of any answering whatsoever** | Take away questioning and you take away understanding, *in every instance* | **No.** It is necessary, a **"transcendental condition of the possibility"** of understanding (Kant). *Transcendental* just means what makes something possible |

## 3. Prejudices are historically transmitted presuppositions (pp. 54–59)

The transcendental conditions are **universal**. Each act of questioning is **particular**. **We can only *think* universals and only *sense* particulars**: we can think "red" but only see *this* red thing.

### The One and the many

- Inherited by Plato from **Parmenides and Heraclitus**. Socrates asks *what the Just itself is*, gets **examples**, and rejects them.
- **Buddhist** ***pratītya-samutpāda*** (interdependence): many entities, yet all are one in conditions and relations (Thich Nhat Hanh).
- **Shankara vs. Ramanuja**: absolute monism vs. **qualified non-dualism**.
- What *grounds* our readiness to see things in their questionability is **the "effect of history."** Like sensation, history **individuates** (Aristotle; MacIntyre).

### What makes *specific* questions possible?

**We can't question what we're totally unfamiliar with.** It doesn't stand out. Some things stand out from the **lifeworld** (the total framework of significance in which a person acts, thinks, and feels) as **question-worthy**. Four sources:

#### (a) Traditions of inquiry → disciplines

- Inherited sets of questions and topics **settle into disciplines**. Disciplines help us be critical (Ch. 1). **"Academic disciplines discipline thinking."**
- There's been an **explosion of disciplines**, e.g., a UK interdisciplinary MA devoted to **the Beatles**.
- **Psychology** example: the subject is the psyche or mind. Its questions include "Is the mind distinct from the brain?" and "Do behaviors indicate mental states?" Its **boundaries** are porous: asking about governance moves you into political science or sociology, and *social psychology* straddles the line.
- **Fields vs. disciplines:** **religious studies** and **African-American studies** are *intrinsically interdisciplinary*. No single method dominates.

#### (b) Cultural and ethnic forms of life

- Traditions involve **sedimentation** (continuity) **and innovation**. Grounding in tradition **frees** us to invent (MacIntyre 1988).
- **Language games and fields of intelligibility** accompany every form of life and set a **horizon of relevance**.
- **Space:** "Where's the restaurant?" "To the right" (egocentric). For Muslims, the ***Qibla*** (direction of Mecca) coordinates space differently, so the predicative possibilities differ.
- **Time:** "2021 CE" is a secularized *Anno Domini*, still counting from Jesus's birth. Dickman says secular users are implicitly complicit in "**Christian supremacy**." The Jewish year is **AM 5781**. Which is the "true" year? Each is true **within its discursive conventions and field of intelligibility** (truth returns in Part II).

#### (c) Historical circumstances

- Some pressing topics seem **cross-cultural**, like how to respect and care for **elders**.
- Some are **era-specific**: after modern science arose in Europe, how to trust science once religion seemed unreliable.
- Some are **local**: directions, or the best pancakes in town.
- Communities define **horizons of inquiry and meaning** and shape **where we expect answers to come from**.

**This is the structure in which "prejudice" occurs.** Stereotypes are only one negative case. **We are preloaded with anticipations of meaning in all experience.**

> **Gadamer:** "History does not belong to us; we belong to it. … The focus on subjectivity is a distorting mirror. … That is why the **pre-judgments** of the individual, far more than [one's] judgments, constitute the historical reality of [one's] being."

#### (d) Provoking experiences

- Something out of the ordinary surprises us: "What is that smell?" "Is it about to rain?" "Was it something I ate?" (Small wonder some people become hypochondriacs.) Climate change: "What's causing the shrinking tundra?"
- **Questions mediate between experience and thought.** When an experience stands out, it is already being **raised into a question**. **Thinking brings experience "back down to earth"** by settling it with a complete thought.

#### (e) Changes in our capacity to perceive

- **Sapir-Whorf hypothesis:** claimed that words *determine perception* (Inuit snow terms).
- Current view: **words determine or encourage *attention***, not perception.
  - **Guugu Yimithirr** (Australia) uses only **cardinal directions**. Speakers seem to point "at" themselves but are pointing **through** themselves (Deutscher 2010).
  - **"Blue" in the ancient world:** the Hebrew Bible and Greek epic describe sky and sea as black, gray, or wine-red/purple. Did people not *perceive* blue, or not *attend* to it? Either way, few ancient questions concerned blue. Modern ones abound.

### Why do we question at all? The ontological conditions

Beyond transcendental and historical conditions, an **ontological and existential** question remains: *what about human being makes questioning possible?* Two structures:

1. **Anxiety (*Angst*)**, treated here
2. **The face of the Other**, deferred to Part III

- *Angst* here isn't the DSM condition. There's **no medication for angst**, though people try to evade it through preoccupation and "self-medication" (Heidegger).
- It is the **negativity intrinsic to intentionality** (Merleau-Ponty, de Beauvoir).

| Reading of angst | Questioning becomes | Failure to get answers feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **Deprivation / lack** (e.g., Sartre) | **Deficit-driven**, a compulsive quest for answers | Loss, failure |
| **Opening / clearing** (Dickman) | **Surplus-driven**, a *desire and love for more* | Nothing lost. A **clearing for growth**, expanding horizons |

Example: asking "what direction am I facing?" and getting answers from very different forms of life (north, toward Mecca) **opens ever-expanding horizons**.

## 4. Dogmatic thought lacks genuine questions (pp. 60–65)

**"Forgetfulness of questioning":** without questions, **thought and sentences dissolve into a mere exchange of signs**. Questioning is easy to forget **because questions don't *say* anything. Answers do.**

### The hegemony of the sign: two biases

#### Bias 1: logic and basic reasoning (propositionalism)

- Logic courses focus on **propositions**: arguments as premises + conclusion, bound by **entailment** and **inferential commitments**.
- But **answers aren't *inferred* from genuine questions**, only from epistemic imperatives (Ch. 2).
- Premises and conclusions **cut loose from their questions become contextless propositions**. **Meyer:** this represses questioning through constant abstraction into **meaningless sentences**. Meyer's name for this bias is **"propositionalism" / propositionalist ideology**.
- Results:
  - "**Trigger-happy fallacy identification and accusation**" come to count as "critical" thinking.
  - Beginners analyze **validity without understanding the propositions**.
  - Sentences are treated as **bigger signs or labels**, true if they "correspond" to a fact and false if not (early Wittgenstein).

#### Bias 2: popular culture and social media

- **Emoji** keep multiplying (smiling poop, octopus, shrug, hearts) to represent ever subtler feelings (Giannoulis & Wilde 2019).
- **Image-centered platforms**: Tumblr, Instagram, Snapchat, Flickr, Pinterest; video on YouTube, Hulu, Netflix. "A picture is worth a thousand words."
- **Passive reactions**: like, dislike, ignore, and now "love" (Seargeant 2019). Users are **placated into passive consumption**.
- Social media *can* be productive (the **Arab Spring**; Alsaleh 2015). But the constant focus on images **restrains thinking in complete thoughts** and builds **habits of attention**. Could we sit through one of **Lincoln's** speeches, some up to **eight hours** long?

### An old idolatry

- People have privileged images since cave-dwelling (icon panels, comic strips).
- The **god of Abraham** forbids idols, even ones meant to represent that god.
- **India**: symbols are trusted as **transparent focal points** for the divine, not the objects of worship (Courtright 1989).
- Compare debates over **video-game violence**: can people tell virtual from actual?
- **Ancient Greece**: did signs relate to things **naturally** (onomatopoeia, "buzz") or by **arbitrary convention**?

**Diagnosis:** is a sign more like a subject or a predicate? **A subject.** The drift toward signs and images is a **drift toward sentential subjects**. **Laying out subjects, however ornately, isn't thinking yet.** Thinking means **putting subjects in predicaments, answering questions actually asked.**

- On Instagram, *what question does each image answer?* Images **don't have to** answer questions. Not everything is an answer, and we shouldn't "smear understanding across everything."
- **The criticism isn't of social media as such**, but of its **surplus**: its **corrosive habits** on thinking complete thoughts. "How can we think complete thoughts in the midst of bombardment by images demanding our loyalty and worship?"

### Two consequences of privileging the sentential subject

**(1) Semiotics overreaches**

- **Semiotics** studies signifiers, signifieds, and referents (Saussure → Peirce → Derrida). It now competes with Marxism and Historicism as the fundamental discourse of intelligibility in the academy.
- It is valuable, from a semiotics of peacock feather displays to tracing signifiers of white supremacy.
- **The problem:** it claims to be the **first and final layer of analysis** and doesn't recognize its limits. It treats complete thoughts as **just another sign**, subordinating **semantics** to **semiotics**. "Perhaps just more sophisticated idolaters?"

**(2) A "glacial drift" toward substance metaphysics**

- Is reality made of **discrete things** (atoms) or **processes and relations** (quantum swerves)?
- It serves the ideology to make us **uninterested** in thinking this through. We're trained to want quick answers and to **stockpile subjects**.
- Most Westerners lean toward **substance metaphysics**: reality as a **box full of piles of things**. ("There have to be things before there can be relations, right?")
- **Political danger:** substances that "truly are one way" have justified claims of **natural inferiority** ("Boys will be boys"), **social Darwinism**, and **race theories** used to rationalize colonization and brutality.
- **But political worries don't make it false.** They can *motivate* asking about the alternative, a **process- or relation-centered metaphysics** (Ronkin 2009).
- **Parallel:** just as **questioning has priority over answering**, **relations have ontological priority over things**.
- **Not deliberate:** ideologies serve upper-class interests (Marx), e.g., the **divine right of kings**. There were about as many gods as rulers. Dickman points away from **individual blame** and toward structures, like **Ibram X. Kendi's** focus on **racist policies** over racist people (2019).

### The forgetfulness of questioning

- We've **forgotten to question**. Questioning seems to have **come to an end**.
- **DRIP** again: the problem isn't too little information. **We don't know what questions the data is supposed to answer.** Much of it is **a stockpile of subjects**, not complete thoughts.
- Compare "**human resources**" (Heidegger): a reservoir of power squeezed from human beings. Or **collectors** who never play, read, or listen to what they acquire. *Whose interests* does the stockpile serve?

### Why questioning is forgettable

- **Questions don't say anything.** They make considering meanings possible but don't **express** a meaning (Schumann & Smith).
- To understand a question is to **ask** it. There's **no tentative attitude** of questioning.
- Genuine questions **occur to us**. They aren't intentional decisions.

### Questions before, after, and about thoughts

> **Genuine questions are language on the way to becoming complete thoughts.**

- The most basic question: **"What is that?"**
- There are questions **on the way to** thoughts, questions **on the other side of** thoughts, and questions **about** thoughts.

### The end of thinking: pro-position, belief, dogma

- **Thinking is self-cancelling**, like most living processes (Nietzsche). **The end of thinking is belief in, or commitment to, a position**: what a "**pro-position**" should mean.
- **Belief becomes dogmatic** when **all possible questioning is policed and repressed**.
- **Belief can be reopened** to more thinking if further questions are allowed.
- **The hegemony of signs and substance metaphysics are complicit with dogmatic belief.**

### Thinking as inner dialogue

- We can't think everything we know at once, so we **draw it out bit by bit**. **Thinking is a dialogue of the soul with itself** (Plato; Gadamer).
- **Are we asking ourselves (and others) genuine questions, or using epistemic imperatives to command and control others and ourselves?** This points ahead to **Part II (reasoning)** and **Part III (dialogue)**.

## Chapter summary in one paragraph

A complete thought's unity comes from a predicate **disclosing** a subject's essence, not from attaching a property to a referent. A question presents the **possibilities** of that unity: it is a **neutralized judgment** suspended between yes and no. We can question a complete thought from **four directions**: its **predicate** (predicament-centered), its **subject**, its **copula** (grounds and justification), or **our own questioning** (the questioner's existential abeyance, which undoes the ego's claim to be an origin). What we're conscious of in questioning is a subject matter's **questionability** (*Frageverhalt*). Questioning has **hermeneutic priority**: it is the **transcendental condition** of understanding anything, which is why a page can go unread when we aren't asking its questions. Which questions occur to us depends on **prejudices**, historically transmitted presuppositions coming from disciplines, forms of life, historical circumstance, provoking experiences, and habits of attention. Ontologically, questioning is made possible by **angst**, read as a **clearing for growth** rather than a lack. When questioning is forgotten, the **hegemony of the sign** takes over: propositionalism in logic, image-saturation in media, overreaching semiotics, and a drift toward **substance metaphysics**, all of which are **complicit with dogma**. Thinking is the soul's dialogue with itself, and it stays alive only while genuine questions are allowed.

## Instructor's suggestions (Appendix, pp. 215–216)

Alongside the ["three things"](../00-Word-Phrase-Sentence-Strategy/README.md) protocol:

1. **Practice the four orientations**: **predicate-centered, subject-centered, copula-centered, questioning-centered.** Use the *Schoolhouse Rock* song **"The Tale of Mr. Morton"** to prime students on the grammatical parts of sentences. Pick sentences, perhaps at random, and illustrate each orientation.
2. **Think, pair, share:** *Can we pose a question without meaning it? Can we understand a meaning (in the technical sense of a complete thought) without meaning it?*

### Practice template: four orientations

Take the sentence **"Your keys are on the kitchen counter."**

| Orientation | Sample question |
|---|---|
| Predicate-centered | Where are my keys? |
| Subject-centered | What's on the kitchen counter? |
| Copula-centered | Are my keys *really* on the counter? How do you know? |
| Questioning-centered | Why am I so anxious to find them, and am I listening to you or just waiting to look? |

## Discussion questions

- Think of a page you recently "read" without understanding. What question was it answering?
- What is your own field's "*Frageverhalt*"? Which subject matters does it make questionable, and which does it leave unquestioned?
- Is your calendar's year "true"? What does your answer assume about truth?
- When does a belief stop being a pro-position and become dogma?
