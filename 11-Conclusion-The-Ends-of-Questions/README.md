## The big idea

> "I do not have a conclusion, as if I could end a book on questioning with a final solution."

Dickman **declines to "conclude"** in the propositionalist sense. He also declines to end with **applications** (therapy, classroom discussion). Applications would make genuine questioning **merely instrumental**, as if it were worthwhile only for the social or natural profit it produces. He ends instead with a **reflective meditation on the joy of endless questioning.**

The title's **"End(s)"** carries two meanings. *End* is a **termination**, which Dickman refuses. *Ends* are **purposes**: joy, life, solidarity, freedom.

---

## 1. The question mark as a symbol

- The Introduction asked whether the **question mark** could serve as a symbol of ultimate concern for a "temple of questioning." The Conclusion takes that up again. The question mark's symbolic value is its **openness toward broadening horizons of understanding**.
- **Through questioning we enhance and amplify our existence.**
- As a symbol, the question mark can represent a **religious-like community of inquirers**, however widely scattered across the earth.
- **Questioning enables confidence and faith. It is not their enemy** (Tillich, *Dynamics of Faith*). **Questioning is a vital expression of faith.**
- Communities that suppress questions tend toward **fanaticism, literalism, exclusivism, and complicity with oppression and exploitation**.

### Case study: the United Methodist Church (2019)

- In **2019** the **UMC** voted to **maintain and more strongly enforce** its ban on people in open non-heteronormative relationships holding leadership positions. Its stated reason was that "**homosexuality is incompatible with Christian teaching**" (UMC *Book of Discipline*).
- Some had already been ordaining people **regardless of orientation or identity**, bypassing the order.
- **Dickman's analysis:**
  - The statement doesn't say "**some**" Christian teaching, or "**Methodist** interpretations." It claims to speak for **"Christian teaching"** as such.
  - It presents itself as a **final answer**, one that "**does not respond to questions but gets rid of questions**."
  - That raises a question. The **United Church of Christ (UCC)** also claims to be Christian and sees **no incompatibility**. Does the UMC mean to say it doesn't recognize the UCC as Christian?
  - **The ban directly inhibits questioning.** After the vote, UMC ministers were **explicitly told not to speak on the issue from the pulpit**.

### Anxious guilt vs. joy

- Final conclusions and the discouragement of questioning produce **anxiety and guilt** in people who **can't quiet their questions**. They see hypocrisy or contradiction in their community or its leaders: "*Our community teaches love, but all we seem to do is express hate toward those who are different from us.*"
- **The book offers a different stance: take joy in questioning** instead of feeling anxious guilt about it. **Stop tying ourselves compulsively to answers** and to a desperate need for "*the*" answer, and **open to the further resources that renewed questioning discloses**.

## 2. Eschatological patience: dwelling with questions

| **Apocalyptically impatient** | **Eschatologically patient** |
|---|---|
| Treats bewilderment as something to end *now* | **Relaxes** into daily questioning |
| Prays daily for the ultimate end of questions | Accepts the **continuing tension** of living with unfolding questions |
| Demands immediate resolution | **Dwells** with questions, seeking ever-broadening horizons |

- **Relaxing into questioning resembles Pyrrhonist *ataraxia*** (tranquility; Thorsrud 2009).
  - Pyrrhonists question our **pretense to answers**: the belief that we must have all the answers and that every question must be answered.
  - They **suspend both belief and disbelief**.
  - **Suspending the compulsion to believe brings liberation.**

> **"Questions are a reality we can affirm with a resounding 'Yes!'"**
> **Taking joy in our questions is life-affirming. Compulsively trying to end questions is life-denying.**
> **Joyously affirming endless questioning lets us pass from death to life.** Our dialogues might go on without end, "in a surplus of understanding."

### Role model: Augustine's *Confessions*

- Dickman offers the Platonist Christian theologian **Augustine** as a model of joyous questioning in Western traditions.
- Religious studies scholar **Charles Mathewes** (2002) argues that Augustine's choice to include the **second half of Book 10 and Books 11–13** shows that for Augustine "**questioning… is not simply a prolegomenon to faith or praise but, in fact, a vital expression of it.**"
- Many readers expect the *Confessions* to end at the **conversion in Book 9**. **Some editors simply cut the later books.**
- In Mathewes's reading, **Augustine's story is about learning to ask questions the right way**, and he gets there only in the later books:
  - Augustine moves from "**apocalyptically impatient**" to "**eschatologically patient**." He drops the habit of demanding complete comprehension and quick resolution.
  - He learns to "**endure**" the "**continuing tension**" of living with unfolding questions. That endurance isn't something "one suffers regretfully. It is, rather, **a mark of being alive**."
  - "To come to see the **joyous endlessness** of such questioning, and to begin to inhabit it, is to **pass from death into life**."
- **Like Augustine**, we undergo questions as they occur to us. We literally **"suffer"** them, in the old sense of *undergo*. They aren't something to regret or get rid of. **They are how we express and enhance our experience of being alive.**

## 3. Neutrality toward finality

- **Neutrality** in questioning (Ch. 2) means more than not biasing answers. It also means being **neutral toward finality**.
- Dickman's study of dialectics opens up **movement without totalizing completion**. Completion can work as a **regulative ideal** we strive toward, but **we don't need to demand that it happen here and now**.
- **Give up the compulsive need for immediate gratification through completion.**
- **Open questions cultivate openness**: to further experience, more dialogue, and greater understanding.

### The seesaw, one last time

```
                questions & responses: the to-and-fro
         ◀──────────────────────────────────────────────▶
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  player                                         player  │
   └────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                                ▲
                         SUBJECT MATTER  (fulcrum: regulates and guides)
```

- Realizing the **perfect game of seesaw**: the subject matter is the fulcrum, and questions and responses are the shifts of weight that make the motion fluid.
- **Like good actors who disappear into their characters, the conversation players disappear and the subject matter "speaks volumes through them."** We want to find out what ***it*** has to say to and through us.

## 4. Statistics can never be dialectics (Ellul and Sartre)

### Against the demand for the "right" answer

- **Jacques Ellul** (*The Technological Society*, 1964) criticizes the natural-scientific attitude for privileging **mathematical precision** to the point that "**only that is knowable which is expressed (or, at least, can be expressed) in numbers**."
- In Ellul's account, which Dickman draws on closely:
  - To escape the "arbitrary and subjective" (ethical and literary judgments, dismissed as "trivial and unfounded"), the "true scientist" retreats to **numbers and statistics**.
  - **Example:** the purely qualitative statement "**the worker is fatigued**" seems to yield nothing. Once **biochemistry measures fatigue numerically**, the fatigue "counts," and a solution seems possible.
  - Yet **the largest realm of technique's effects can't be reduced to numbers**, and it gets dismissed as **pseudo-problems or as nonexistent**.
  - Everyday versions: "*I don't do 'qualitative' research*" and "*That's too deep for me*." The "scientific" position often **denies the existence of whatever falls outside current, narrowly construed method**.
  - Because the industrial machine's problems are almost entirely numerical, **all of technique gets unintentionally reduced to a numerical question**.

### Statistics vs. dialectics

Following Ellul, Dickman extends **Sartre**'s claim that "**statistics can never be dialectics**":

| **Statistics** (and every technique) | **Dialectics** (dialogue) |
|---|---|
| A **univocal** method | Many voices and perspectives |
| Expresses an aspect of reality **combinable with nothing else** except other statistics | Integrates multiple perspectives |
| **Can't tolerate contradiction** or evolutionary development | **Contradiction and evolution are intrinsic** to it and to the fusion of horizons |
| Grasps evolution only **formally**: numerical points connected by **extrapolation**, taking linear formulation as evolution's essence | Grasps the **internal, continuous mechanism of evolution** and **the interplay of negations within affirmations** |
| Proceeds **only by affirmation**, excluding negation, refusal, destruction | Moves through **productive negativity** (*Aufhebung*) |
| A logical evolution of **accumulation** | A dialectical evolution of **change and growth** |

> **Dialogue integrates multiple perspectives not by accumulating ever more refined statistics, but by broadening horizons of understanding.**

## 5. Answers divide, questions unite

- **Diversity of meanings often implies divisiveness**, though it need not. This happens among committed religious people and among people committed to **anti-religiousness**. Each side believes its answers are ***the* answer**.
- Divisiveness can be **terrifying and murderous**. Dickman doesn't rehearse those consequences. It also takes **subtler forms**: the **dismissal of someone's voice** in dialogue, the **exclusion of individuals from communities**.
- **Divisiveness in any form threatens the integrity of humane social life.**

> **"While many answers threaten to divide human beings and generate various states of alienation, genuine questions promise to bring human beings together in solidarity while simultaneously disclosing our primordial belongingness within the world and with one another."**
> **"Questions can only be genuine, really, when shared."**

## 6. Dialogue as pointless, joyful freedom

- **Dialogue, as living language, isn't a means to a precalculated end.** It isn't a tool for getting something, such as transmitting one's will.
- It is **a life process** in which we **cultivate and develop our being in a world**, a common ground that supports both individual and collective efforts.
- **It has no point the way other actions do. The process itself seems to be the point.** (Compare Ch. 7: the mode of being of play is self-presentation. Ch. 8: "dialogue presents dialogue.")

### Freire: rigor and joy

> "It is also false to consider seriousness and joy to be contradictory, as if joy were the enemy of methodological rigor. On the contrary, **the more methodologically rigorous I become in my questionings and in my teaching practice, the more joyful and hopeful I become** as well. **Joy does not come to us only at the moment of finding what we sought. It comes also in the search itself.**"
> (Freire 2001, 125)

### The book ends on questions

Dickman closes the book with three questions instead of a thesis:

1. **How might seemingly pointless practices like dialogue help liberate us from the hegemony of instrumental calculation?**
2. **What is the point of doing something pointless?**
3. **Is freedom when we find joy in pointlessness?**

## Closing reflection prompts

- Which of Dickman's three closing questions would you most want to dwell with, without answering it?
- Name a community you belong to that discourages a particular question. What would asking it together look like?
- Where in your life do you behave "apocalyptically impatient" toward a question? What would eschatological patience look like there?
- After working through the whole book, is the question mark a symbol you'd accept? Why, or why not?
